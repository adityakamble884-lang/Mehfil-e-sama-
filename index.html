The Essence of Mehfil-e-Sama

Discover the 700-year living tradition where sacred poetry, rhythmic clapping, and devotional music unite the seeker with the Divine.

✦ ✦ ✦

Mehfil (محفل / महफ़िल) originates from the Arabic word meaning an intimate gathering. In the Chishti Sufi order of the Indian subcontinent, a Mehfil-e-Sama is not mere entertainment; it is an act of spiritual listening (Sama)—a vehicle for deep contemplation, detachment from worldly pride, and awakening of Divine love.

"Sama is the hearing of that which cannot be said, and the saying of that which cannot be heard."

— Traditional Sufi Adab

The tradition was born in the 13th century through the spiritual synergy of Delhi's revered saint, Hazrat Nizamuddin Auliya, and his devoted poet-disciple, Hazrat Amir Khusrau. Khusrau fused classical Persian poetic meters with the indigenous folk melodies of Hindavi and Braj Bhasha, creating the musical architecture of Qawwali that continues to reverberate across shrines from Ajmer to Lahore.

Every track on Mehfil is curated to recreate that tranquil courtyard at midnight—surrounded by glowing lamps, flickering candles, and the persistent, heartbeat-like rhythm of the dholak.

🥁

The Rhythm of Dhikr (Taali & Dholak)

The characteristic synchronized clapping (Taali) in Qawwali mirrors the dual beats of the human heart, continuously repeating the inner remembrance of God.

🪗

The Harmonium & Drone

The sustained reed drone creates a hypnotic acoustic foundation, freeing the lead singer (Ustad) to soar into improvised devotional poetry and high octaves.

🕊️

Wajad & Spiritual Ecstasy

When the poetry resonates deeply with the listener's longing, it leads to Hal (a spiritual state) and Wajad (ecstasy)—the transcendence of individual ego.

Pillars of Qawwali

The master voices who carried the sacred fire across generations and across continents.

✦ ✦ ✦

Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan

شہنشاہِ قوالی
Shahenshah-e-Qawwali

Possessor of an extraordinary vocal range and stamina, Ustad Nusrat introduced Qawwali to global audiences, performing with ecstatic devotion and unprecedented musical improvisation.

Signature: "Yeh Jo Halka Halka Suroor Hai"

The Sabri Brothers

صابرِی برادران
Masters of Chishti Sama

Led by Haji Ghulam Farid Sabri and Maqbool Ahmed Sabri, their resonant baritone voices and dramatic pauses set the benchmark for traditional shrine performances worldwide.

Signature: "Bhar Do Jholi Meri"

Abida Parveen

ملکۂ صوفیانہ کلام
Queen of Sufi Music

The undisputed doyenne of mystical music, renowned for her thunderous renditions of Sindhi, Punjabi, and Persian verses by Bulleh Shah, Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai, and Rumi.

Signature: "Chaap Tilak" & "Tu Jhoom"

Rahat Fateh Ali Khan

وارثِ نصرت
Torchbearer of Gharana

Trained directly by his uncle Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan from the age of seven, he bridges classical gharana virtuosity with modern cinematic ballads and Coke Studio masterpieces.

Signature: "Afreen Afreen" & "O Re Piya"

Fareed Ayaz & Abu Muhammad

قوال بچّوں کا گھرانہ
Qawwal Bachhon Ka Gharana

Direct musical descendants of Hazrat Amir Khusrau's original 13th-century ensemble, preserving rigorous Persian, Urdu, and Purbi classical grammar with unsurpassed purity.

Signature: "Ghar Nari Ganwari" & "Shikwa"

Aziz Mian Qawwal

فلسفیٔ قوالی
The Philosopher of Qawwali

Master of dramatic arguments with the Almighty, rapid-fire declamatory poetry, and deeply intellectual discourse exploring human destiny and divine justice.

Signature: "Main Sharabi" & "Teri Soorat"

Hazrat Amir Khusrau (1253–1325 CE) was a polymath, Sufi mystic, poet, and musician who lived in Delhi as the prime disciple of Hazrat Nizamuddin Auliya. He synthesized Persian modal systems (Maqam) with Indian classical ragas to invent the Qawwali vocal format as an accessible devotional medium. He is also credited with developing Hindustani musical instruments like the sitar and tabla.
